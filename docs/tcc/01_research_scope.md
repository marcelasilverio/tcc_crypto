# Research scope

## Working title

*Machine Learning Applied to Cryptocurrency Portfolio Selection*.

## Research problem

The study asks whether a machine-learning-based model used to support
cryptocurrency portfolio selection can produce attractive financial results.
The motivation is that cryptocurrency markets combine continuous trading,
high volatility, fast information circulation, speculative behavior, and
sensitivity to technological, regulatory, behavioral, and macroeconomic
events. These conditions increase the difficulty of investment decisions and
make the market a useful setting for quantitative decision support.

The manuscript frames the problem through bounded rationality. Human decision
makers cannot process all available information, while machine learning may
help identify patterns in large and nonlinear datasets. The proposed system is
an aid to human decision-making, not a substitute for it.

## General objective

Analyze the applicability of a machine-learning algorithm to cryptocurrency
portfolio selection.

## Specific objectives

- Describe the cryptocurrency market, with emphasis on volatility and the data
  available for decision-making.
- Analyze how portfolio-selection theory, especially the mean-variance model,
  behaves in the cryptocurrency setting alongside machine learning.
- Evaluate a supervised Support Vector Machine classifier using historical
  cryptocurrency market data.

## Intended contribution

The work connects three bodies of knowledge:

- cryptocurrency markets;
- Modern Portfolio Theory;
- supervised machine learning, centered on Support Vector Machines.

The intended practical contribution is a structured, data-informed process
that first identifies candidate assets and then allocates capital among them.
The intended academic contribution is evidence about whether supervised
classification can complement traditional portfolio selection in a volatile,
emerging asset class.

## Research characterization

The manuscript characterizes the study as:

- **applied**, because it develops and evaluates a procedure for a practical
  decision problem;
- **quantitative**, because it manipulates numerical market data and uses
  statistical, classification, and financial metrics;
- **descriptive**, because it describes and evaluates model and portfolio
  behavior without claiming a causal explanation;
- **bibliographic and documentary**, because it combines academic literature
  with secondary historical records obtained from a public exchange API.

## Scope boundary

TCC I defines a proposed experiment. It does not report trained models,
backtests, statistical tests, or conclusions about financial performance.
Agents must not describe expected benefits as observed results.
